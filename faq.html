A drone flight over the site generates a 3D surface model, which is used to calculate the volume of stockpiled material. This is useful for tracking material on site, verifying deliveries, or reconciling against invoices.

Yes, when ground control points (GCPs) are available. Two flights are taken at different times, each generating a 3D surface, and the surfaces are compared to show where material was cut or filled across the site. GCPs surveyed by the site's own surveyor are needed to align the two flights accurately. If GCPs aren't available on a given site, stockpile volume reporting is the better fit for tracking material.
